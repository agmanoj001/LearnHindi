# Making it world class, offline first

This review (November 2026) asks what would make Khel Khel Mein stand beside the best children's reading apps, with one firm rule: **everything must keep working with no internet** once the app is on the device. ✅ marks what is done; ➡️ is recommended next, in priority order inside each part.

## Where it stands

The app is already strong on what the research rewards most: short, active lessons, adaptive difficulty, spaced review, retelling, a daily limit, no ads and no data leaving the device (see [LEARNING-REVIEW.md](LEARNING-REVIEW.md)). When this roadmap was first written it was weakest where top apps spend their money (the voice, the art, the reading itself).

Since then, the voice (recorded narration, a voice per comic character, voices for every lesson), the stories (moving scenes, reading on their own) and offline safety (backups, bundled downloads) have been built. What is left, from a full design review of the app's screens (November 2026), is mostly **how it looks and feels**: the layout of lessons, the life in the characters, a consistent language, and the engineering habits of a professionally run app. Sections 4–8 are that review; the first items of each are now built (marked ✅). A second review (November 2026) looked at what would bring a child back day after day and at what the app teaches: it added items 7 to 16 in section 7, and sections 9 (Hindi stories and poems to add) and 10 (maths to add).

| ✅ | **Scenes for Grades 3 and 4, calmer and fewer**, so older readers aren't distracted. The validator holds Grade 3–4 stories to these rules (`calmProblems` in js/scene.js), and to a scene on at most half their pages | Older readers still enjoy a moving picture at the start and the end of a story, but motion on every page pulls the eye away from longer text | None |

## 2. Offline that never lets a family down

| | What | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ✅ | **Backup and restore** in the grown-ups' area: one small file with stars, settings and children | A lost phone or a cleared browser used to lose every star |
| ✅ | **Shows whether the browser will keep the app's saves**, and asks it to | Browsers can evict data from sites that aren't installed when space runs low |
| ✅ | Installed apps ask to keep their storage at start; recordings ask when saved | (earlier round) |
| ✅ | **Split the content by grade** | `content.json` is 2.2 MB (380 KB compressed), and two thirds of it is the Grade 7–8 comics. A toddler's first open on a slow connection would be about three times faster with a small core file plus grade packs, all still saved for offline. **Done:** `tools/split-content.mjs` splits `content.json` into `content/core.json` (worlds, words, phrases, shows, quizzes: 93 KB compressed) and a pack per grade with its stories, comics, grammar, riddles and idioms (Toddler 7 KB, Kindergarten 40 KB, … Grade 7 145 KB), each named with a hash of its contents. A first open loads the core and the child's grade (for Grades 7–8, Kindergarten's stories too, for the "read to your brother or sister" mission) and shows the home screen; the other packs follow, and any other screen waits for them. The service worker saves the core and every pack, so after that everything loads from the device. A toddler's first open now fetches 100 KB instead of 490 KB; `npm test` fails if `content/` is out of step with `content.json` |
| ✅ | **Share the backup** with the Web Share API (to the family's own WhatsApp or Drive), and offer a reminder once a month | Backups only help if they are made. **Done:** the grown-ups' area has **Share the backup**: the phone's share sheet (the Web Share API) sends the backup file to the family's own WhatsApp, Drive or email. (Android's Chrome shares text files but not .json ones, so the shared file is a .txt holding the same backup, and Restore reads both; where a browser can't share files, the file is saved instead.) It shows when the last backup from this device was made. Once a month, when there are lessons with stars worth keeping, the grown-ups' button on the home screen shows a dot and the grown-ups' area opens with a reminder: share it now, or remind me next week. Nothing is sent anywhere unless a grown-up chooses to share it |
| ➡️ 3 | **Play Store build** (a Trusted Web Activity) and an iOS wrapper | Installed store apps aren't evicted, and families find them where they look. See [PLATFORM-EVALUATION.md](PLATFORM-EVALUATION.md) |
| ✅ | **An airplane-mode check in every release**: install, go offline, play one lesson from each grade, restart, check the stars | Catches a file missing from the offline cache before families do. **Done:** part of every push and pull request (8.1): install the app, let the service worker save it, check that every file it loaded is in the offline cache, go offline, play one lesson from each grade to the end, restart and check the stars were kept; a page error anywhere fails it |

## 4. Layout and ease of use

Found by looking at about 30 screens (Kindergarten and Grade 7 on a phone, Grade 1 on a tablet). Priority numbers show the order to build in.

| | What | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ✅ | **Fill the screen in lessons.** Done: answer cards, pictures, letters and maths numbers now grow with the screen (up to about 250 px on a tablet, 300 px when there are only two answers), and their words grow with them | On a 1024 px tablet the answer cards stay phone-sized (about 60 px wide) in a sea of empty space; on a phone the middle third of most lesson screens is empty and the answers are small. Small targets are the main cause of missed taps for ages 2–5 |
| ✅ | **A simpler home screen for pre-readers.** Done: Toddler and Kindergarten see their guide, one big play button (the next thing on today's path), Show time, Story radio and their own worlds. No filters, skill tags or other grades; a grown-up can switch back with "Simple home screen" in the grown-ups' area | A toddler sees a path card, two big buttons, a row of filter chips and world cards with small labels. None of it can be read at that age, and every extra choice costs attention |
| ✅ | **The counting game**: show the animals on the bus large, and let each one bounce as it is counted. Done: the passengers fill the bus at the biggest size that fits (one is about 150 px on a phone, ten still fit in three rows), and each one hops and wears its number (१, २, ३ or 1, 2, 3) as it gets on, gets off, or is counted at the end | The animals are tiny and hard to count at a glance |
| ✅ | **The hint button in every game**, not only the choose-an-answer games (maths, phonics, digging, tracing, the train). Done: the 💡 now works in maths, phonics and first sounds (a maths problem says its own hint, such as "count on from 5"), and in the games with more than one step it lights the next thing to do: the pile to dig, the letter a word needs, the next word of a sentence, the next picture of a story, a syllable not heard yet, and shining spots on the parts of a letter not traced yet. Where any answer is right (picking a train or a vehicle) it shows one. Two wrong taps in a row bring it without asking, as before | Right now the 💡 is missing in the games that don't use the shared answer handler, which feels inconsistent |
| ✅ | **A 30-second "tap here" guide** the first time each kind of game is played: a pointing hand and the instruction said aloud. Done: the first time a child meets each kind of game, once its question has been said, a hand shows what to do while the guide says it: it moves across the answers ("listen, then tap the right one"), taps the first tile of a word or sentence ("one by one, in order"), taps a pile or syllable ("tap each one"), drags along the letter to trace, or taps the page arrow in a story. It shows once per child (saved with their progress and backups); a grown-up can show it again from the grown-ups' area | Nothing tells a first-time player what to do; the best children's apps teach the interaction with the interaction |
| ✅ | **Move grown-up text off child screens** behind an ⓘ button (for example Show time's "Questions wait a few seconds…" note and the small instruction paragraph on comic covers). Done: one ⓘ button shows or hides the notes for grown-ups on a screen: on Show time (how the two modes work, and what each episode teaches), on Story radio (how the dark screen works), on comic covers (how to use the comic), on the break screen (how to get ten more minutes) and in the "no Hindi voice" card on the home screen (the setup steps). Children see the pictures and titles only | Text a child can't read, or doesn't need, is noise |
| ✅ | **Tablet layouts for the maps and the hub** (two or three columns, bigger world cards). Done: on a tablet the home screen puts today's path (or the big play button) beside Show time, Story radio and Play together, so the worlds start higher up; world cards are bigger and sit two or three across; a world's map has bigger stops (124 px instead of 92) on a wider winding path. Phones keep their layout | The hub and map are phone layouts stretched on tablets |

## 5. Animation and feel

| | What | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ✅ | **Give story characters life.** Done: every face blinks; people's mouths move while a page with speech is read; five moods (happy, sad, surprised, scared, sleepy) change eyes, mouth and arms. Each page gets its mood from its text or its move, and a page can set `mood` itself | Scenes now move a whole picture (slide, hop, spin); the characters have no moving limbs and their faces never change. This is the biggest single jump in animation quality, and it costs only a few KB of SVG |
| ✅ | **Depth in the backgrounds.** Done: Vehicle Town is redrawn in layers (a pale skyline, houses with roofs and windows, trees, traffic both ways); the layers drift at different speeds and follow a finger or a tilt a little; birds, butterflies and falling leaves pass through the outdoor worlds. All of it stands still with "reduce motion" | The backgrounds are flat (plain lavender blocks for buildings, a road strip); a little parallax makes the worlds feel alive |
| ✅ | **A guide that reacts to every answer** (cheers, thinks, encourages, claps) and is bigger on screen. Done: in every lesson the guide thinks along when a question appears (head tilted, hand on chin, 🤔), says "you can do it" after any wrong tap (💪 and a wave; the shared answer handler now reports every wrong tap), claps and jumps for a first-time answer (👏) and cheers one found with help (🎉). It is bigger in lessons: 96 px on a phone, up to 160 px on a tablet. With "reduce motion" the bubbles still show; only the movement stops | The guide sits small in a corner and mostly watches; attachment to a companion is one of the strongest levers on engagement for young children |
| ✅ | **Shared-element transitions**: tapping a world, a lesson or a story grows into the next screen instead of cutting to it. Done: a world card grows into its map, a lesson stop (or today's path, or the big play button) into the lesson, a library book into the story and a Show time card into the episode; going back shrinks the screen into the card it came from. It uses the browser's View Transitions; where they aren't supported, or with "reduce motion", the usual slide-in stays | It makes the app read as one place rather than a set of pages, and it tells a child where they went |
| ✅ | **A motion budget and a slow-phone test**: measure frames per second on an entry-level Android phone and an older iPad, and drop any effect that stutters. Done (docs/MOTION-BUDGET.md): `tools/motion-budget.mjs` measures scrolling, opening a map and a lesson, and answering, with the CPU slowed 4× and 6× like an entry-level phone. The grow transition, drifting backgrounds, ambient birds and leaves, the bobbing guides on the world cards and big sparkle bursts stuttered; a slow device now gets lighter animations without them (found automatically from the first screens' frame times, or chosen in the grown-ups' area). The map was made cheaper for everyone. At 4×, with lighter animations, everything is within budget (50+ frames a second, no frame over 150 ms; the map's worst frame went from 283 ms to 150 ms). The phones were emulated; a check on a real one is still worth doing when one is at hand | Stutter is the first thing that makes a children's app feel cheap; the new scenes and particles have only been checked on a fast machine |
| ✅ | **Hindi text animation**: letters that draw themselves in stroke order for tracing, and syllables that pop as they are said. Done: before a child traces, the letter writes itself with the vehicle as the pen, in the usual order: its body first, then the standing line (खड़ी पाई), then the top line (शिरोरेखा) left to right, then any mark above it. The order is worked out from the letter's own shape (thinned to its centre line), so it works for every letter; a touch stops it. In the बारहखड़ी each syllable pops as it is said, and a word just built is said piece by piece, each piece popping, before the whole word ("ब… स… बस") | Hindi conjuncts and matras are shapes children must see built; this turns reading support into delight |
| ✅ | **Haptics and sound polish**: a distinct, soft sound per interaction type, and a quieter mix under speech everywhere. Done: seven kinds of interaction each have their own soft sound and their own vibration, the same all over the app: a tick for round buttons (back, the speaker, the 💡), a pop for picking something with no wrong answer, a wooden "tok" when a tile or word drops into its slot, a chime for a right answer, two soft falling notes for a wrong one (it used to make no sound), a jingle for a level up or a run of right answers, and a page sound. Under speech the music drops further on every screen (to 15% outside lessons, 8% in them) and the sound effects drop to half, so a tap never covers a word | Sound is half the feel; a few consistent tones help non-readers know what just happened |

## 6. Text, language and voice

| | What | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ✅ | **One language rule, everywhere.** Done for the home screen and the world maps: with Hindi on, everything a child reads is Hindi; with "both", Hindi first and English small after it (English first in the maths and phonics worlds). Child-facing text follows the language setting, and never English alone. Today the home screen mixes them ("Keep going / Rhymes / Story time", the skill tags and some world names are English only, while titles are Hindi) | A child who is learning Hindi shouldn't have to switch languages to find the play button; mixed screens also read as unfinished |
| ✅ | **Fix the wrong or awkward captions.** Done: "Find the cup house", "Help! Find the cow", "{word} for a boy. And for a girl?", and Hindi word order ("गाय कौन बोलता है?", "बड़ा कौन सा है?"); "भाग" for a unit, as on the map. The changed lines were recorded again, and a device that saved the old recordings forgets them | The caption bar quotes the app's own prompts; small slips here are the most visible in screenshots and reviews |
| ➡️ 3 | **More praise, mostly for effort**: ten or more lines instead of two (शाबाश!, वाह!), such as "तुमने कोशिश की!", "ध्यान से सुना!", "अब और अच्छा!" | Praising effort and strategy builds persistence far better than praising being right (Mueller and Dweck); repeating the same two lines becomes noise |
| ➡️ 4 | **Re-record the very short lines inside a carrier phrase** (single letters, *है*, *Yes*) so one narrator voice says everything | About 40 lines (those the premium voice returned as silence) use a different, standard voice, so a few moments sound like a different person |
| ➡️ 5 | **A native-speaker review by a Hindi teacher** of all stories and prompts, and a spot-check of the voices, especially names and nasal sounds | All the Hindi was written by the build tool and the voices are computer-made; a teacher will catch phrases a parent would find odd |
| ➡️ 6 | **A style guide for the words**: everyday Hindi for ages 2–8, school terms in brackets from Grade 3, one spelling for each word, and a short glossary the voice, captions and stories all follow | The "everyday Hindi" pass found bookish words in many places; a guide keeps new content from drifting back |
| ➡️ 7 | **Voice speed for beginners**: a slower setting for Toddler and Kindergarten, applied on top of the recorded voice | A child learning a sound needs more time between words than an older reader does |
| ➡️ 8 | **Google voice for every word the app says**: every story, prompt and comment, every Hindi and every English word, spoken by the Google text-to-speech voice, including **all** the maths sections, so the phone's own voice is never heard. The recordings are made ahead of time and kept on the device, so this still works with no internet. Today the stories, comics, Show time and the fixed lines of the Hindi lessons are recorded. The maths questions, though, are made up as the child plays, with new numbers, answers, hints and "why" lines each time, so nearly all of them use the device voice: a sample of 15,471 maths lines found only 68 recorded. The talk-together prompts have no voice at all. What is left: record every line the maths games can say (or build each line from recorded pieces), give the talk prompts a voice, and add a check that fails when any line a child can hear has no recording | The device voice differs from phone to phone, often has no Hindi, and sounds robotic beside the narrator; one voice for everything sounds finished, and a child learning the sounds of a language should always hear them said the same, good way |

## 7. Engagement

| | What | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ✅ | **Make collections playable.** Done: a collection opens as its world's picture with a tray of earned stickers. Tap or drag a sticker in; drag it around (lower is nearer, so bigger); tap it to hear it; drag it out to take it away. Animals blink and bob. The picture is saved with the progress and in backups; the album button still shows every sticker | A collection that can be used is worth coming back for, and it gives the stars a purpose a child can see |
| ✅ | **Favourites and "read again"**: a heart on any story, and the favourite shows on the home screen. Done: every book in the library and the results screen after a story have a heart; the guide says "यह कहानी पसंदीदा में आ गई!". The newest four favourites wait on the home screen under "फिर से पढ़ो · Read again", one tap from reading (the book grows into the story). Favourites are kept with the child's progress and backups | Young children love repetition; letting them find a favourite in one tap is the best retention feature they ask for |
| ✅ | **Story radio**: a sleep timer, a way to pick a story from picture tiles, and "play this one again". Done: "कहानी चुनो" opens every story as a picture tile (the one playing is marked), and a tap plays it; ↻ plays the current story again from the start; the 😴 sleep timer goes off → at the end of this story → 10, 20 or 30 minutes, showing the time left. When it runs out the guide says "शुभ रात्रि!", the screen goes dark and the phone may sleep; play starts the radio again | The radio is the most-used screen on a journey and at bedtime, and it has no way to choose or to stop on its own |
| ✅ | **A gentle daily rhythm**: a "today's three" view with a small celebration at the end, and a week-long sticker trail. Done: today's path has at most three steps and now stays the same all day (before, a step played was swapped for the next lesson, so the day could never be finished). When the last one is done, the home screen celebrates (confetti, "आज के तीनों पूरे!") and the day's sticker flies onto a Monday-to-Sunday trail shown on the today card and the simple home screen, with an extra cheer for a full week. Missing a day loses nothing | There is a daily path, but the end of the day has nothing to look forward to; a visible finish makes short sessions satisfying |
| ✅ | **Little rewards that aren't stars**: a new guide outfit, a new backdrop, a new sound. Done: as the stars add up (10, 20, 30, 45 … 350), the child unlocks 14 prizes in turn: five outfits every guide can wear (a party hat, a crown, a flower, a bow, a marigold garland), five home-screen backgrounds (garden, ocean, space, snow, a fair) and four button sounds (bell, wood block, bubble, chime). The results screen announces a new one with a "use it" button, and "मेरे इनाम · My prizes" on the home screen shows them all, the next one to look forward to, and lets the child switch any time. Nothing can be bought or lost | Variety in what is earned keeps a long-running app fresh |
| ✅ | **Shared play for a grown-up and child**: prompts like "ask me why" after a story, and a two-player mode on one device for brothers and sisters. Done: the talk-together prompts after every story (1.4), and "साथ खेलो" on the home screen. Two players (any child on the device, or a grown-up) take turns for 10 rounds, each at their own level: find the picture for Toddler and KG, read the word for Grades 1–2, that grade's quizzes for Grades 3–8, and a Hindi number word past 20 for the grown-up. Every right answer adds a star to one shared jar, and the other player may help | Co-viewing and conversation add far more to learning than the app can on its own |
| ✅ | **Sing-along rhymes**: the rhymes play as songs with a bouncing ball over the words (the word timings from section 1 make this possible), a clap-along beat and actions the guide does. Done: every rhyme in the reader is sung along by default: a ball hops onto each word as the recording reaches it (from the word timings), a soft clap lands on every word, the guide claps and dances along, and the world's tune plays under the words like a song instead of ducking away. "🎵 गाओ" on the page switches back to plain reading (remembered); reading alone ("मैं ख़ुद पढ़ूँ") never sings | Song is how small children hold on to language; there are 11 Hindi and 8 English rhymes today, all spoken, none sung |
| ✅ | **Make-your-own story**: in the sticker picture, the child arranges the stickers, presses record and tells what is happening; the picture and their voice are saved as their own book in the library. Done: "🎙️ मेरी कहानी" in the sticker picture opens the picture with a big microphone (up to a minute); the child hears it back and keeps it or tries again. Kept books sit on a "मेरी किताबें · My books" shelf at the top of the library, and a tap plays the picture with their voice while the stickers bob. The picture is kept with the progress; the voice stays in the device's recordings, like every family recording | Creating beats consuming for engagement and for speaking practice, and it reuses two things already built (sticker scenes and the recorder) |
| ✅ | **An older child reads to a younger one**: a "read to your brother or sister" mission in Grades 7–8 that walks the older child through recording a Kindergarten story, which then plays for the younger child with a "read by…" ribbon. ## What not to do

- **No cloud accounts or online analytics.** Trust is a feature; the privacy-first design is part of what makes this app different.
- **No video files for stories.** CSS scenes give most of the delight at a thousandth of the size, and they stay sharp on every screen.
- **No speech recognition yet.** In browsers it needs the internet, and offline models are too large for this audience's phones.
